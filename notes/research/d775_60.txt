===== POST id=26256 number=69 time=2026-01-12T20:59:46+00:00
DNV [https://forum.dudu-auto.com/d/775-rfi-canbus-decoder-w-best-support-and-its-uart-byte-sequence-mappings/68]
 

To sniff CAN bus traffic and upload custom firmware to your CANBUS decoder (e.g., OD-OLO-02 or other CANbox), the high-level steps are:


- Capture raw CAN traffic: Use a CAN interface + sniffer (e.g., Arduino Due with a CAN transceiver such as MCP2515, see my post above) to read messages from the canbus on your car. Log IDs and data fields while exercising controls you care about (SWC buttons, parking sensors, etc.). You can use my arduino sketch I linked for that. This gives you the raw CAN frames that represent real car signals.
 


- Map signals to UART sequences: Analyze those CAN frames and decide which bits/bytes correspond to the functions you want (e.g., pick-up, hang-up, back, enter, speed, sensors). Translate those into the UART protocol that the head unit expects. You can reference existing protocols like Raise/VW/PQ or Raise/Toyota/Rav as a template.



- Write/modify firmware: Take open firmware sources for an STM32-based decoder (e.g., the canbox-xc90-my2008 project on GitHub). Integrate your CAN ID handlers and UART output mapping so that when a particular CAN frame arrives, the firmware emits the corresponding UART byte sequence.



- Flash the CANBUS decoder: Open the decoder box, identify the MCU (often STM32). Connect a programmer (like ST-Link V2) to SWD pins (SWDIO, SWCLK, 3.3 V, GND). Use the programmer to upload your compiled firmware binary to the decoder.  ￼



- Configure the head unit: In Settings → Vehicle → CANBUS, set the protocol to the one your firmware is emulating (for example Raise/VW/PQ). Test and iterate — adjust your firmware if the head unit doesn’t react as expected.



In short: you first sniff and understand CAN messages, then map them to the HU’s UART protocol, and finally flash that logic into the decoder hardware (CANbox) so that the head unit gets meaningful UART signals. The specific sequence values depend on the UART protocol you choose to emulate.  ￼

I assume you mean how do you map CAN ID messages and payloads to the signals they represent?


You can either have a DBC file from the OEM that explains what message ID + payload bits mean what on the canbus of your car. The DBC files are rarely made public though.
 

Your other option is to reverse engineer the CAN messages of your car using this: https://github.com/erik-rosen/can_sniffer_tools [https://github.com/erik-rosen/can_sniffer_tools] with and Arduino Due with a CAN transceiver and and ODB connector.
